# Internal Communications Skill

## Overview

This skill provides comprehensive guidance for creating professional, effective internal communications across various formats and contexts. It covers everything from weekly status reports to company-wide announcements, with ready-to-use templates and best practices for clear, engaging communication.

## Core Communication Principles

### 1. Know Your Audience
- Identify the primary and secondary audiences
- Understand their information needs and preferences
- Adjust technical depth and formality accordingly
- Consider different communication styles (executives vs. engineers vs. operations)

### 2. Lead with Impact
- Put the most important information first (inverted pyramid)
- Use clear, concise headlines
- Provide executive summaries for longer communications
- Make action items immediately visible

### 3. Be Clear and Actionable
- Use specific, concrete language
- Define clear next steps and owners
- Include deadlines and timelines
- Avoid jargon unless audience-appropriate

### 4. Show Progress with Data
- Use metrics to demonstrate impact
- Provide context for numbers (trends, comparisons)
- Visualize data when possible
- Balance quantitative and qualitative information

### 5. Balance Transparency with Tact
- Be honest about challenges and setbacks
- Frame problems with potential solutions
- Acknowledge uncertainty when appropriate
- Celebrate wins without exaggeration

### 6. Make Content Scannable
- Use clear headings and subheadings
- Employ bullet points and numbered lists
- Highlight key information with bold or color
- Keep paragraphs short (3-4 lines max)

## Communication Types

### Status Reports

**Purpose**: Provide regular updates on progress, challenges, and priorities.

**Frequency Options**:
- Daily: Standup summaries, quick wins
- Weekly: Team/project progress, blockers
- Monthly: Comprehensive updates, trend analysis
- Quarterly: Strategic reviews, OKR progress

**Standard Structure**:
```
1. Executive Summary (1-2 sentences)
2. Key Metrics & Progress
3. Accomplishments/Wins
4. Challenges & Blockers
5. Upcoming Priorities
6. Help Needed
7. Resources & Links
```

**Best Practices**:
- Keep weekly reports under 500 words
- Use consistent format and timing
- Include specific metrics and outcomes
- Link to detailed documentation
- Highlight cross-team dependencies

**Common Formats**:
- Engineering: Sprint progress, technical debt, incidents
- Product: Feature launches, user feedback, roadmap updates
- Operations: Process improvements, efficiency metrics
- Leadership: Strategic initiatives, organizational changes

### Company Newsletters

**Purpose**: Build company culture, share news, and recognize achievements.

**Frequency**: Weekly, bi-weekly, or monthly

**Standard Sections**:
```
1. From Leadership (optional, monthly)
2. Company Updates & Milestones
3. Team Spotlights & Recognition
4. New Hires & Announcements
5. Upcoming Events
6. Learning & Development
7. Fun Section (photos, memes, celebrations)
```

**Best Practices**:
- Keep it visual and engaging
- Mix serious and fun content
- Feature different teams/people each issue
- Include photos and graphics
- Make it mobile-friendly
- Maintain consistent branding
- Track open rates and engagement

**Tone Guidelines**:
- Warm and conversational
- Inclusive and celebrating diversity
- Authentic, not corporate-speak
- Balance professionalism with personality

### All-Hands Announcements

**Purpose**: Communicate important company-wide information requiring immediate attention.

**Use Cases**:
- Major organizational changes
- New policies or procedures
- Security incidents or urgent issues
- Strategic pivots or major decisions
- Significant customer/business wins
- Leadership changes

**Standard Structure**:
```
1. Clear Subject Line (action-oriented)
2. TL;DR Summary (2-3 bullet points)
3. Context & Background
4. The Announcement (what's changing)
5. Why This Matters
6. What Happens Next (timeline)
7. Action Items (if any)
8. FAQ Section
9. Contact for Questions
```

**Best Practices**:
- Send at consistent times (e.g., Tuesday 10am)
- Use clear, direct language
- Anticipate questions with FAQ
- Provide multiple channels (email, Slack, meeting)
- Follow up with smaller group discussions
- Archive announcements for later reference

**Tone Guidelines**:
- Professional and authoritative
- Transparent and honest
- Empathetic to concerns
- Forward-looking and optimistic

### Team Updates

**Purpose**: Keep team aligned on progress, learnings, and priorities.

**Frequency**: Weekly or bi-weekly (aligned with sprints)

**Standard Structure**:
```
1. Sprint/Period Summary
2. Wins & Accomplishments
3. Key Metrics
4. Learnings & Retrospective Items
5. Upcoming Work
6. Team Health & Morale
7. Shout-outs & Recognition
```

**Best Practices**:
- Rotate who writes/presents updates
- Include team member contributions
- Be honest about challenges
- Celebrate small wins
- Document lessons learned
- Share with adjacent teams
- Keep format consistent

**Formats**:
- Written updates in Slack/email
- Video recordings for distributed teams
- Slide decks for team meetings
- Shared documents for async updates

### Policy & Procedural Updates

**Purpose**: Communicate changes to company policies, processes, or procedures.

**Critical Elements**:
```
1. What's Changing (clear summary)
2. Effective Date
3. Why It's Changing (rationale)
4. Who It Affects
5. What Action Is Required
6. Where to Find More Information
7. Transition Plan (if applicable)
8. FAQ Section
```

**Best Practices**:
- Provide advance notice (2-4 weeks when possible)
- Explain the "why" clearly
- Offer training or support resources
- Create visual guides or flowcharts
- Send reminders before effective date
- Collect and address feedback
- Update handbook/wiki immediately

**Sensitive Topics Guidance**:
- Benefits changes: Extra clarity, comparison charts
- Remote work policies: Address equity concerns
- Performance reviews: Emphasize fairness and transparency
- Compensation changes: Detailed rationale, individual conversations

### Change Management Communications

**Purpose**: Guide organization through significant changes with clear, supportive communication.

**Phases**:

**1. Pre-Announcement (Internal Prep)**
- Align leadership team
- Identify stakeholders and concerns
- Prepare FAQ and talking points
- Plan communication cascade

**2. Initial Announcement**
- Clear explanation of change
- Honest rationale
- Expected timeline
- Support resources available

**3. Ongoing Updates**
- Regular progress reports
- Address emerging concerns
- Celebrate milestones
- Adjust based on feedback

**4. Post-Implementation**
- Lessons learned
- Success metrics
- Thank yous and recognition
- Documentation updates

**Best Practices**:
- Over-communicate rather than under-communicate
- Use multiple channels and formats
- Allow time for questions and processing
- Acknowledge emotional responses
- Provide specific examples and scenarios
- Empower managers with talking points
- Track sentiment and address concerns quickly

**Communication Frequency During Change**:
- Major changes: Daily or every 2-3 days initially
- Medium changes: Weekly updates
- Minor changes: Bi-weekly check-ins

### Recognition & Celebrations

**Purpose**: Acknowledge achievements, milestones, and contributions to build culture.

**Types**:
- Individual achievements (promotions, anniversaries, wins)
- Team accomplishments (project launches, goals met)
- Company milestones (funding, revenue, customer wins)
- Personal celebrations (birthdays, life events)

**Standard Format**:
```
1. Exciting headline
2. What happened/was achieved
3. Why it matters
4. Who was involved (credit everyone)
5. Impact or outcomes
6. Congratulations and thanks
```

**Best Practices**:
- Be timely (celebrate soon after achievement)
- Be specific about contributions
- Include photos or visuals
- Share widely (don't silo recognition)
- Encourage peer-to-peer recognition
- Balance individual and team recognition
- Be genuine and authentic

**Channels**:
- Slack #wins or #announcements channel
- Company newsletter feature
- Team meetings shout-outs
- Email to relevant groups
- Social media (with permission)

### Incident Communications

**Purpose**: Provide clear, timely updates during and after incidents.

**During Incident**:
```
1. Incident Detected (initial notification)
   - What we know
   - Current impact
   - Team responding
   - Next update time

2. Progress Updates (every 30-60 minutes)
   - Current status
   - What we've tried
   - Expected resolution time
   - Impact changes

3. Resolution Notice
   - Incident resolved
   - Final impact summary
   - Post-mortem timeline
```

**Post-Incident**:
```
1. Post-Mortem Report
   - Executive summary
   - Timeline of events
   - Root cause analysis
   - Impact assessment
   - What went well
   - What could improve
   - Action items and owners
   - Prevention measures
```

**Best Practices**:
- Designate a communication lead
- Use consistent update schedule
- Be honest about uncertainty
- Avoid blame in initial communications
- Provide technical and non-technical summaries
- Follow up on action items publicly
- Share learnings company-wide

### FAQ Documents

**Purpose**: Proactively address common questions about policies, changes, or initiatives.

**Structure**:
```
1. Introduction/Context
2. General Questions
3. Specific Topics (grouped logically)
4. Who to Contact for More Help
5. Last Updated Date
```

**Best Practices**:
- Organize questions by theme
- Use clear, searchable question phrasing
- Keep answers concise (2-3 paragraphs max)
- Link to detailed documentation
- Update regularly based on new questions
- Include "What if" scenarios
- Make it easy to find (wiki, pinned message)

## Structure and Format Guidelines

### Executive Summaries

**Purpose**: Allow busy readers to grasp key information in 30 seconds.

**Format**:
```
## Executive Summary

**TL;DR**: [One sentence key takeaway]

**Key Points**:
- [Most important point with metric/outcome]
- [Second most important point]
- [Third most important point]

**Action Required**: [Specific next step, if any]
```

**Best Practices**:
- Keep to 3-5 bullet points maximum
- Lead with outcomes, not activities
- Include specific numbers when relevant
- Make it self-contained (readable without rest of document)
- Use bold for critical information
- Place at the very top of document

### Key Metrics and Dashboards

**Presenting Metrics**:
```
### Metrics Overview

| Metric | Current | Previous | Target | Status |
|--------|---------|----------|--------|--------|
| [Metric 1] | [Value] | [Value] | [Value] | 🟢/🟡/🔴 |
| [Metric 2] | [Value] | [Value] | [Value] | 🟢/🟡/🔴 |

**Status Key**: 🟢 On track | 🟡 At risk | 🔴 Off track
```

**Best Practices**:
- Show trends, not just point-in-time data
- Provide context (why metrics matter)
- Use consistent time periods
- Include targets or benchmarks
- Explain significant changes
- Use visual indicators (colors, icons)
- Link to detailed dashboards

### Accomplishments Section

**Structure**:
```
## Wins & Accomplishments

### Major Achievements
- **[Achievement]**: [Impact/outcome] ([Metric if applicable])
  - Context: [Why this matters]
  - Team: [Who contributed]

### Additional Wins
- [Accomplishment with brief impact statement]
- [Accomplishment with brief impact statement]
```

**Best Practices**:
- Lead with impact, not activity
- Quantify results when possible
- Credit team members by name
- Connect wins to company goals
- Balance big and small wins
- Use specific examples

### Challenges and Blockers

**Structure**:
```
## Challenges & Blockers

### Critical Blockers 🔴
- **[Blocker]**: [Impact if not resolved]
  - **Help Needed**: [Specific ask]
  - **Owner**: [Person working on it]
  - **Due**: [When resolution needed]

### Risks & Challenges 🟡
- **[Challenge]**: [Current mitigation approach]
```

**Best Practices**:
- Be honest and direct
- Provide proposed solutions or next steps
- Indicate urgency clearly
- Make asks specific and actionable
- Include what you've already tried
- Don't bury critical issues
- Follow up on resolution

### Upcoming Priorities

**Structure**:
```
## Upcoming Priorities

### Next Week/Sprint
1. **[Priority 1]** (Owner: [Name])
   - Why: [Strategic importance]
   - Success criteria: [How we'll measure]

2. **[Priority 2]** (Owner: [Name])
   - Why: [Strategic importance]
   - Success criteria: [How we'll measure]

### Future (Next 2-4 weeks)
- [Upcoming initiative]
- [Upcoming initiative]
```

**Best Practices**:
- Limit to top 3-5 priorities
- Assign clear ownership
- Explain strategic rationale
- Define success criteria
- Show how priorities connect
- Update consistently

### Call-to-Action Items

**Format**:
```
## Action Items

| Action | Owner | Due Date | Status |
|--------|-------|----------|--------|
| [Specific action] | @name | YYYY-MM-DD | [Status] |
| [Specific action] | @name | YYYY-MM-DD | [Status] |

**Need help with**: [Specific requests for support]
```

**Best Practices**:
- Make actions specific and achievable
- Assign single owner (not team)
- Include realistic due dates
- Track status visibly
- Follow up on completions
- Link to related work

### Resource Links

**Format**:
```
## Resources & Links

**Related Documents**:
- [Project Charter](link)
- [Technical Design Doc](link)
- [Dashboard](link)

**Context**:
- [Previous Status Report](link)
- [Roadmap](link)

**Discussions**:
- Slack: #channel-name
- GitHub: [Issue #123](link)
```

## Tone and Style Guidelines

### Professional Yet Approachable

**Do**:
- Use conversational but clear language
- Write like you speak (but edited)
- Use contractions appropriately
- Show personality within bounds
- Be warm and human

**Don't**:
- Use corporate jargon or buzzwords
- Write in overly formal or stiff language
- Use unnecessary complexity
- Be artificially cheerful about problems
- Sacrifice clarity for cleverness

**Examples**:

❌ "We are pleased to announce that the strategic initiative has reached its preliminary milestone."

✅ "Great news! We've hit our first major milestone on the customer portal redesign."

❌ "Stakeholder engagement has been suboptimal, resulting in potential timeline impacts."

✅ "We're behind schedule because we haven't gotten feedback from the marketing team yet. We need their input by Friday to stay on track."

### Transparency and Authenticity

**Do**:
- Share both good news and challenges
- Admit when you don't know something
- Explain the reasoning behind decisions
- Acknowledge different perspectives
- Show real progress, not just activity

**Don't**:
- Spin bad news into forced positivity
- Hide problems until they're critical
- Pretend to have all the answers
- Ignore valid concerns
- Exaggerate accomplishments

**Examples**:

❌ "While we encountered some timing challenges, we're excited about the learning opportunity this presents."

✅ "We missed our deadline by two weeks due to unexpected technical complexity. Here's what we learned and how we'll adjust our estimates going forward."

❌ "Everything is on track and going great!"

✅ "We launched on schedule (great!), but we're seeing lower adoption than expected. We're investigating why and will share findings next week."

### Inclusive Language

**Do**:
- Use gender-neutral language (they/them for singular)
- Consider diverse backgrounds and perspectives
- Avoid idioms that don't translate well
- Be mindful of cultural differences
- Use accessible language (avoid ableist terms)
- Consider time zones for global teams

**Don't**:
- Make assumptions about experience or background
- Use military or sports metaphors exclusively
- Reference culture-specific holidays/events only
- Use unnecessarily gendered language
- Use phrases like "obviously," "simply," "just"

**Examples**:

❌ "Hey guys, let's circle back and take this offline."

✅ "Hi team, let's continue this discussion in a smaller group."

❌ "As you all obviously know..."

✅ "As a reminder, our policy is..."

❌ "We're going to war with this competitor."

✅ "We're focusing our efforts on competitive differentiation."

### Action-Oriented Messaging

**Do**:
- Use active voice
- Start with verbs
- Make requests specific
- Set clear deadlines
- Define ownership
- Show clear next steps

**Don't**:
- Use passive voice excessively
- Be vague about expectations
- Leave actions unassigned
- Set unrealistic timelines
- Assume people know what to do

**Examples**:

❌ "A decision needs to be made about the framework."

✅ "Sarah, please decide which framework we're using by Friday."

❌ "Please review when you get a chance."

✅ "Please review by end of day Tuesday and leave comments in the doc."

❌ "We should probably think about addressing this."

✅ "Let's schedule 30 minutes this week to create an action plan."

### Appropriate Formality by Context

**Formal** (All-hands announcements, policy changes, external stakeholders):
- Complete sentences and proper grammar
- Professional tone throughout
- Careful word choice
- Minimal emoji or casual elements
- Edited and polished

**Semi-Formal** (Weekly status reports, team updates, most internal comms):
- Clear and professional but conversational
- Personality appropriate
- Occasional emoji for clarity or tone
- Contractions acceptable
- Efficient rather than elaborate

**Informal** (Slack messages, quick updates, team chat):
- Conversational and brief
- Emoji and GIFs appropriate
- Fragments acceptable
- Quick and efficient
- Personality encouraged

### Cultural Sensitivity

**Do**:
- Be aware of global holidays and working hours
- Avoid idioms that don't translate
- Consider language barriers
- Be explicit rather than implicit
- Provide context for cultural references

**Don't**:
- Assume everyone celebrates the same holidays
- Use region-specific references without explanation
- Schedule all-hands during obvious conflicts
- Use humor that doesn't translate
- Reference politics or controversial topics

## Detailed Workflows

### Creating Regular Status Reports

**Step 1: Preparation (5 minutes)**
- Review previous status report
- Check metrics dashboard
- Review completed work (tickets, PRs, documents)
- Note any blockers or risks
- Identify upcoming priorities

**Step 2: Draft Content (15 minutes)**
- Start with executive summary (write this last)
- Fill in metrics with context
- List accomplishments with impact
- Document challenges honestly
- Outline next priorities
- Add action items with owners

**Step 3: Add Context (10 minutes)**
- Explain significant metric changes
- Provide links to relevant documents
- Credit team members by name
- Connect work to company goals
- Add helpful screenshots or visuals

**Step 4: Review and Refine (5 minutes)**
- Check for clarity and conciseness
- Ensure action items are specific
- Verify all links work
- Proofread for errors
- Confirm consistent formatting

**Step 5: Distribute (2 minutes)**
- Send at consistent time
- Post in relevant Slack channels
- Tag relevant stakeholders
- Archive in shared location
- Add to status report index

**Time Commitment**:
- Weekly: ~30-40 minutes
- Becomes faster with consistent format
- Can reduce to 20 minutes once routine

### Crafting Engaging Newsletters

**Step 1: Content Collection (Ongoing)**
- Set up submission form for content
- Monitor Slack channels for wins
- Track launches and milestones
- Collect photos and media
- Note upcoming events
- Solicit team spotlights monthly

**Step 2: Content Selection (20 minutes)**
- Review all submitted content
- Choose diverse content (different teams/topics)
- Prioritize time-sensitive items
- Balance serious and fun content
- Select strong visuals

**Step 3: Writing and Design (45 minutes)**
- Write compelling headlines
- Craft engaging opening
- Write each section with personality
- Add images and graphics
- Ensure mobile-friendly layout
- Include clear calls-to-action

**Step 4: Review Process (30 minutes)**
- Get leadership approval (if needed)
- Verify all facts and dates
- Check all links work
- Test on mobile device
- Proofread carefully
- Get second set of eyes

**Step 5: Distribution and Follow-up (15 minutes)**
- Send at optimal time (Tuesday-Thursday morning)
- Post excerpts in Slack
- Pin for visibility
- Track open and click rates
- Solicit feedback
- Archive for future reference

**Time Commitment**:
- Weekly newsletter: ~2 hours
- Bi-weekly newsletter: ~3 hours (more content)
- Monthly newsletter: ~4 hours (more comprehensive)

**Optimization Tips**:
- Use templates to speed up design
- Batch write multiple sections
- Delegate section ownership
- Reuse format and structure
- Build content library over time

### Writing Clear Announcements

**Step 1: Preparation**
- Clarify the decision/change to announce
- Understand the rationale deeply
- Identify all affected groups
- Anticipate reactions and concerns
- Gather supporting data/documentation
- Align with leadership team

**Step 2: Structure the Message**
- Write TL;DR first (the headline)
- Outline key sections
- Plan FAQ section
- Determine appropriate length
- Choose tone and formality level

**Step 3: Draft Content**
- Start with clear, direct summary
- Provide necessary context
- Explain the what, why, and when
- Address "what this means for you"
- Include specific next steps
- Add FAQ section
- Provide contact for questions

**Step 4: Anticipate Reactions**
- Consider different perspectives
- Address concerns proactively
- Prepare managers with talking points
- Plan for follow-up discussions
- Have escalation plan for issues

**Step 5: Review and Approve**
- Get leadership sign-off
- Legal review (if needed)
- HR review (if people-related)
- Test on sample audience
- Finalize timing

**Step 6: Multi-Channel Distribution**
- Email to all-company
- Post in Slack #announcements
- Discuss in all-hands meeting
- Publish on company wiki
- Schedule manager talking points
- Plan follow-up communications

**Step 7: Monitor and Respond**
- Watch for questions and concerns
- Respond quickly and thoughtfully
- Collect feedback
- Adjust messaging if needed
- Schedule follow-up update

**Critical Timing Considerations**:
- Avoid Friday afternoons (gets lost over weekend)
- Avoid Monday mornings (competes with week planning)
- Best: Tuesday-Thursday 10am-2pm
- Allow time for questions before end of day
- Consider global time zones

- Highlight most impactful work

**Rotating Ownership**:

**Template Customization**:
- Engineering: Focus on technical achievements, tech debt, incidents

### Crisis Communication Protocols

**Incident Communication Framework**

**Phase 1: Detection (First 5 minutes)**

Initial Message Template:
```
🚨 INCIDENT DETECTED

**Status**: Investigating
**Impact**: [Current known impact]
**Started**: [Time]
**Team**: [Who is responding]

We're investigating [brief description]. Next update in [timeframe].
```

**Phase 2: Active Response (Every 30-60 minutes)**

Update Template:
```
📊 INCIDENT UPDATE [#N]

**Status**: [Investigating/Identified/Fixing/Monitoring]
**Impact**: [Current impact and scope]
**Progress**: [What we've learned/tried]
**ETA**: [Expected resolution time, if known]

Next update: [Time]
```

**Phase 3: Resolution (Once resolved)**

Resolution Template:
```
✅ INCIDENT RESOLVED

**Resolution Time**: [Duration]
**Final Impact**: [Summary of impact]
**Cause**: [Brief root cause, if known]
**Next Steps**: Post-mortem in [timeframe]

Thank you for your patience.
```

**Phase 4: Post-Mortem (Within 2-5 days)**

Full post-mortem following blameless format:
- Timeline of events
- Root cause analysis
- Impact assessment
- What went well
- What could be improved
- Action items with owners
- Follow-up date

**Communication Roles**:
- **Incident Commander**: Makes technical decisions
- **Communications Lead**: Writes updates (dedicated role)
- **Customer Communications**: Handles external updates
- **Executive Liaison**: Briefs leadership

**Best Practices**:
- Stick to regular update schedule
- Be honest about uncertainty
- Don't speculate on causes initially
- Keep technical and exec summaries separate
- Archive all communications
- Follow up on all action items

**Methods for Gathering Communication Feedback**

```
Communication Effectiveness Survey:

1. Do you feel well-informed about company direction? (1-5)
2. Are status reports useful and relevant? (1-5)

6. What topics need more communication? (Open-ended)

```

**2. Engagement Metrics**
- Email open rates
- Newsletter click-through rates
- Slack reactions and replies
- Wiki/documentation page views
- All-hands meeting attendance
- Question volume after announcements

**3. Direct Feedback Channels**
- Regular 1-on-1s with reports
- Team retrospectives
- Skip-level meetings
- Town hall Q&A sessions
- Dedicated feedback Slack channel
- Communication office hours

**4. Response Time Tracking**
- Time to acknowledge questions
- Time to provide full answers
- Clarity of initial responses
- Need for follow-up clarification

**5. Content Analysis**
- Questions asked after announcements
- Topics requiring most clarification
- Recurring confusion points
- Information gaps

**Acting on Feedback**:
- Share feedback summary with team
- Identify top 3 improvement areas
- Create action plan with owners
- Implement changes incrementally
- Measure impact of changes
- Close the loop on what changed

## Best Practices by Medium

### Email Communications

**When to Use**:
- Formal announcements
- Policy changes
- Detailed explanations
- External stakeholder communications
- Archival importance
- Need for receipts/records

**Best Practices**:
- Subject line: Clear, specific, action-oriented
- Keep under 500 words when possible
- Use bullet points and formatting
- Put most important info first
- Include clear next steps
- Send at optimal times (Tues-Thurs, 10am-2pm)
- Avoid Reply All when possible
- Use BCC for large lists
- Provide opt-out for non-essential lists

**Subject Line Formulas**:
- Action-oriented: "[Action Required] Submit expenses by Friday"
- Update: "[Update] Q4 Planning Schedule"
- FYI: "[FYI] New parking policy effective 1/1"
- Question: "[Input Needed] Feedback on office hours"

### Slack/Chat Communications

**When to Use**:
- Quick updates
- Team coordination
- Informal announcements
- Real-time collaboration
- Building culture
- Quick questions

**Best Practices**:
- Use threads to keep channels organized
- Post in appropriate channel (not DM for team info)
- Use @here and @channel sparingly
- Include context in your message
- Use emoji for tone and reactions
- Pin important messages
- Set reminders for follow-ups
- Use Slack workflows for recurring updates

**Channel Organization**:
- #announcements: Company-wide important news
- #general: General discussion, team bonding
- #wins: Celebrating achievements
- #team-[name]: Team-specific updates
- #project-[name]: Project coordination
- #help-[topic]: Getting assistance

### Wiki/Intranet Documentation

**When to Use**:
- Long-term reference material
- Policies and procedures
- Onboarding information
- Team directories
- Living documents
- How-to guides

**Best Practices**:
- Create clear information architecture
- Use templates for consistency
- Include table of contents
- Add last updated date
- Link related documents
- Make it searchable
- Regular audits for outdated info
- Clear ownership for each page

**Essential Pages**:
- Company handbook
- Team org charts and contacts
- Communication guidelines
- Common FAQs
- Process documentation
- Project archives
- Meeting notes

### Meeting-Based Communications

**When to Use**:
- Complex topics requiring discussion
- Change management
- Team alignment

## Measuring Communication Effectiveness

### Key Metrics to Track

**Engagement Metrics**:
- Email open rates (target: >60%)
- Click-through rates (target: >20%)
- Newsletter subscription rates
- Slack reaction counts
- Meeting attendance rates
- Wiki page views

**Comprehension Metrics**:
- Questions asked after announcements
- Support ticket volume related to comms
- Survey scores on clarity
- Rework due to miscommunication
- Time to decision/action after communication

**Sentiment Metrics**:
- Employee satisfaction with communication (survey)
- Trust in leadership communication
- Feeling of being informed
- Communication as factor in engagement scores

**Efficiency Metrics**:
- Time spent writing communications
- Time from draft to publish
- Channels used per message type
- Redundant communications sent

### Communication Audit Process

**Quarterly Review**:
1. **Inventory all communication channels and cadences**
   - What are we sending?
   - How often?
   - To whom?

2. **Assess effectiveness**
   - What's working well?
   - What's creating noise?
   - What's missing?

3. **Gather feedback**
   - Survey employees
   - Review metrics
   - Conduct focus groups

4. **Make adjustments**
   - Consolidate redundant comms
   - Add missing communications
   - Change frequency or format
   - Sunset ineffective channels

5. **Document and communicate changes**
   - Share what you learned
   - Explain changes
   - Set new expectations

## Common Pitfalls and How to Avoid Them

### 1. Information Overload

**Problem**: Too much communication, drowning out important messages

**Symptoms**:
- Declining open rates
- Complaints about "too many emails"
- Important messages getting missed
- People tuning out announcements

**Solutions**:
- Consolidate into digests
- Use tiered importance (need-to-know vs. FYI)
- Establish communication hierarchies
- Respect "no meeting" times
- Create clear signal-to-noise ratio
- Sunset unused channels

**Prevention**:
- Ask "Does this need to be a separate message?"
- Use appropriate channels (wiki vs. email vs. Slack)
- Set communication calendars
- Batch related updates

### 2. Burying the Lede

**Problem**: Most important information hidden in middle or end of message

**Symptoms**:
- Questions about info you already shared
- Low engagement with critical information
- Confusion about what action is needed
- Having to re-send with clearer subject

**Solutions**:
- Use inverted pyramid structure
- Write TL;DR at the top
- Use clear, specific subject lines
- Put action items in visible locations
- Use formatting to highlight key info

**Prevention**:
- Write summary first
- Ask "What does the reader most need to know?"
- Test your message on someone unfamiliar with topic
- Use the "so what?" test

### 3. Inconsistent Formatting

**Problem**: Each communication looks and reads differently

**Symptoms**:
- Readers don't know where to find information
- Takes longer to scan and understand
- Looks unprofessional
- Harder to create and write

**Solutions**:
- Create and use templates
- Establish style guide
- Use consistent tools and platforms
- Train team on formats

**Prevention**:
- Document your templates
- Review for consistency before sending
- Use automation where possible
- Make templates easy to access

### 4. Lack of Clear Action Items

**Problem**: Readers don't know what they need to do

**Symptoms**:
- Questions like "What should I do?"
- Missed deadlines
- No response to requests
- Confusion about ownership

**Solutions**:
- Always include explicit action items section
- Use format: Action | Owner | Due Date
- Tag people directly (@mentions)
- Follow up on action items

**Prevention**:
- Ask "What action am I requesting?"
- Make deadlines specific
- Assign single owners (not teams)
- Make action items visible (formatting)

### 5. Missing Context

**Problem**: Assumes too much background knowledge

**Symptoms**:
- Many clarifying questions
- Misunderstanding of purpose
- Confusion about relevance
- "Why are we doing this?"

**Solutions**:
- Include background section
- Link to previous communications
- Explain the "why"
- Define acronyms and jargon
- Provide relevant history

**Prevention**:
- Write for the least informed audience member
- Have someone unfamiliar review
- Include "Background" or "Context" section
- Link to related documents

### 6. Overly Technical Jargon

**Problem**: Using language audience doesn't understand

**Symptoms**:
- Blank stares in meetings
- Questions about terminology
- Miscommunication across teams
- Exclusion of non-technical stakeholders

**Solutions**:
- Define technical terms
- Use analogies and examples
- Create glossaries
- Provide multiple explanation levels
- Link to documentation

**Prevention**:
- Know your audience
- Write at appropriate level
- Use "explain like I'm 5" test
- Get feedback from diverse reviewers

### 7. Irregular Communication Cadence

**Problem**: No predictable schedule for updates

**Symptoms**:
- Surprise announcements
- Missed information
- Stakeholder confusion
- Lack of trust

**Solutions**:
- Establish regular schedules
- Send even when "nothing to report"
- Use calendar invites for recurring comms
- Set expectations for frequency
- Stick to the schedule

**Prevention**:
- Create communication calendar
- Set reminders for regular updates
- Batch create multiple updates
- Delegate when you're unavailable

### 8. One-Way Communication

**Problem**: No opportunity for feedback or questions

**Symptoms**:
- Confusion and rumors
- Resistance to changes
- Lack of engagement
- Feeling of disconnect

**Solutions**:
- Always include how to ask questions
- Hold follow-up Q&A sessions
- Monitor Slack for questions
- Conduct pulse surveys
- Create feedback mechanisms

**Prevention**:
- Build in two-way channels
- Respond to questions promptly
- Acknowledge feedback received
- Close the loop on actions taken

### 9. Ignoring Different Communication Styles

**Problem**: Using only one medium or format

**Symptoms**:
- Some people always seem uninformed
- Complaints about communication methods
- Unequal access to information
- Declining engagement

**Solutions**:
- Use multiple channels for important messages
- Offer sync and async options
- Provide written and verbal versions
- Accommodate different time zones
- Make content accessible (captions, transcripts)

**Prevention**:
- Consider audience diversity
- Offer multiple formats
- Ask about preferences
- Track which methods work best

### 10. Lack of Follow-Through

**Problem**: Communications without accountability or updates

**Symptoms**:
- Action items not completed
- Lack of closure on topics
- Decreased trust in communications
- "What happened with that?"

**Solutions**:
- Track action items explicitly
- Send follow-up updates
- Close the loop on initiatives
- Report on outcomes

**Prevention**:
- Set reminders for follow-ups
- Include follow-up dates in original message
- Use project tracking tools
- Establish accountability

## Quick Reference

### Communication Type Decision Tree

```
Need to communicate something?
│
├─ Is it urgent and affects everyone?
│  └─ Yes → All-hands announcement (email + Slack + meeting)
│
├─ Is it a regular update on progress?
│  └─ Yes → Status report (email or doc)
│
├─ Is it celebrating wins or building culture?
│  └─ Yes → Newsletter or recognition post
│
├─ Is it a policy or process change?
│  └─ Yes → Policy announcement with FAQ
│
├─ Is it ongoing crisis/incident?
│  └─ Yes → Incident communication protocol
│
└─ Is it team-specific progress?
   └─ Yes → Team update
```

### Formality Spectrum

```
Most Formal                              Least Formal
│                                               │
└─ Policy changes, legal matters               │
   └─ All-hands announcements                  │
      └─ Executive communications              │
         └─ Regular status reports             │
            └─ Team updates                    │
               └─ Newsletter content           │
                  └─ Slack messages            │
                     └─ Team chat              │
```

### Communication Checklist

Before sending any communication, verify:

- [ ] Audience clearly identified
- [ ] Purpose is clear
- [ ] Most important information is first
- [ ] Action items are specific and assigned
- [ ] Deadlines are included
- [ ] Context is provided
- [ ] Tone is appropriate
- [ ] Grammar and spelling checked
- [ ] Links work
- [ ] Formatting is consistent
- [ ] Channel is appropriate
- [ ] Timing is right
- [ ] Follow-up plan exists

## Conclusion

Effective internal communication is a skill that improves with practice. Start with templates, adapt them to your context, and iterate based on feedback. Remember:

- **Clarity beats cleverness** - Be direct and specific
- **Consistency builds trust** - Regular, predictable communication
- **Context matters** - Always explain the why
- **Two-way is better** - Create space for feedback
- **Less is often more** - Respect people's time and attention

Use this skill as a starting point, customize for your organization, and continuously improve based on what works for your team.
